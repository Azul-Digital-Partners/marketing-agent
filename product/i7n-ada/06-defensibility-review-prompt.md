# 06. Defensibility Review: Instructions to Send With a Sample Report

Use: attach one recent i7n report (PDF or Word) and paste everything below the line into the review chat.

---

You are reviewing an accessibility assessment report as if you were an attorney defending a small business or nonprofit that just received an ADA website demand letter. Your client plans to send this report back to the plaintiff's counsel as evidence of good-faith conformance work.

The report is attached. Review it and answer the questions below. Be direct. Quote the exact text you're referring to.

## 1. Would you send it?

- Would you send this report to opposing counsel as it stands? Yes, yes with edits, or no.
- What's the single biggest reason?

## 2. Statements that hurt the client

List every sentence that could be used against the client or the assessor. For each one:

- Quote it
- Say why it's a problem: false or unverifiable, overstates coverage, implies legal advice, implies a guarantee, claims privilege, contradicts another part of the report
- Give replacement wording, or say "delete"

Check specifically for:

- Claims of testing methods or tools that the report doesn't show evidence of, such as manual screen reader testing
- Claims of "100%" coverage or "complete" assessment
- The word "compliant" or "compliance" used as a result
- Legal statements: safe harbor, settlement figures, privilege, "intended for legal proceedings"
- Grades or scores that invite argument

## 3. Required elements

For each element, mark Present, Weak, or Missing, and say what's needed:

1. Standard and version named: ADA, measured against WCAG 2.2 Level AA
2. Scope: specific URLs, pages, and user flows assessed, and what was out of scope
3. Assessment date
4. Methodology in plain language: automated scan plus manual expert review
5. Findings mapped to specific WCAG success criteria, with severity
6. Expert review notes from a named person
7. Remediation record: what was fixed and when
8. Assessor name and credentials
9. Statement of good-faith conformance that says the report is not legal advice and not a guarantee against claims

## 4. Consistency

- Does the report name one WCAG version consistently, or mix 2.1 and 2.2?
- Do counts, dates, and site names match across sections?
- Does any section contradict another?

## 5. What would make it stronger

Top five changes, ranked by how much each one improves the report's value in responding to a demand letter. Keep each to one or two sentences.

## 6. Format of your answer

- Start with the verdict from section 1.
- Then sections 2 through 5 in order.
- Use short bullets. No preamble.

You are not giving legal advice to anyone. This is a structured critique of a document's credibility.
